    UchQadam

    I launched a hyperlocal grocery delivery service, beat the only alternative 3× on speed and 3× on price, and got zero orders. This is what actually broke.

    Duration
    1 month launched & killed
    Role
    Solo
    Market
    Samarkand, Uzbekistan

    Printed flyers, a Telegram bot, and a partner shop. I set the kill criteria before launch and stopped when the numbers hit them.

    Overview

    People in my neighborhood were already paying a local guy about 5,000 so'm to fetch things from the shop. Nobody had marketed that to them. It was just happening.

    My hypothesis: formalize what already happens informally and the behavior transfers. It didn't.

    0-to-1 Concierge MVP Funnel Analysis Growth Experiment Kill Decision

    The Setup

    One neighborhood, roughly 500 apartments in a 500-metre radius. No hyperlocal delivery existed. The only alternative was Korzinka, a supermarket chain: 45 to 60 minutes, 15,000 so'm.

    Mine was about 15 minutes at 4,900 so'm. I charged on purpose instead of giving it away, because I wanted to see whether convenience was something people would actually pay for.

    What Happened

    Doors reached
    250
    QR scan rate
    12%
    Conversations started
    2
    Orders completed
    0

    Two weeks live, another week trying to fix it, shut down at day 30 against criteria I set before launch.

    Direct mail usually runs 1 to 5%, so attention was not the problem. What broke happened after the scan, at two separate points.

    What I Built

    Flyer with a QR code → Telegram bot → the shop's staff group chat → replies relayed back to the customer. One employee from the partner shop handled delivery. About five hours to build and effectively zero cash.

    Telegram rather than an app or a website. Everyone here already opens it several times a day, so there was nothing to install and nothing to learn.

    Bilingual Uzbek and Russian. Full size: front, back, posters.

    What Broke

    1. The bot asked for a habit people didn't have

    People scanned, hit Start, and landed on a blank text prompt. No catalog, no prices. To order you had to already know what you wanted and type it out. 28 of the 30 people who scanned never typed a word.

    Ordering groceries by typing into a chat is new here. Changing how a neighborhood shops takes months of steady spending on educating people, and I had neither the time nor the money. A catalog in the first message would have lowered the bar, not removed it.

    2. We lost both people who reached out

    Two people got past the prompt and messaged us. The shop did not reply in time.

    That comes down to how I set it up. I handed the customer-facing step to the partner shop and built no way to see or control what happened once a message arrived. The partner had nothing at stake, so when things went quiet there was nothing to push on.

    3. What I still do not know

    The informal version ran on a known person, a neighbor people recognized. Mine ran on a QR code and a bot. I kept the logistics and removed the trust. The shop is also only a 500-metre walk, so convenience may not have been worth much here. Both are plausible and I would want to test each properly.

    What I Take From It

    I wanted to know whether people would pay for convenience, and I got zero transactions, so that question is still open. This was not a negative result. It was no result, and I would rather say that than call it market validation.

    Five hours and effectively no money to rule out a direction I would otherwise have spent a summer on. I set the kill criteria before launching, fewer than five orders a week, and stopped when the data hit them.

    The lesson was not about grocery delivery. Fix the service before the funnel, because a working funnel into a broken service just distributes the failure faster.

    • Run every order myself for the first two weeks, before handing anything off.
    • Knock on twenty doors in week one rather than after the numbers were already in.
    • Put the catalog and prices in the bot's first message. One hour of work.
