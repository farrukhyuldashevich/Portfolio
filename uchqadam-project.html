    UchQadam

    I launched a hyperlocal grocery delivery service in my neighborhood, three times faster and three times cheaper than the only alternative. Thirty people scanned the flyer. Two ordered.

    Duration
    1 month launched & killed
    Role
    Solo
    Market
    Samarkand, Uzbekistan

    Printed flyers, a QR code into Telegram, and a partner shop. I set the kill criteria before launch and stopped when the numbers hit them.

    Overview

    People in my neighborhood were already paying a local guy about 5,000 so'm to fetch things from the shop. Nobody had marketed that to them. It was just happening.

    My hypothesis: formalize what already happens informally and the behavior transfers. It didn't.

    0-to-1 Concierge MVP Funnel Analysis Growth Experiment Kill Decision

    The Setup

    One neighborhood, roughly 500 apartments in a 500-metre radius. No hyperlocal delivery existed. The only alternative was Korzinka, a supermarket chain: 45 to 60 minutes, 15,000 so'm.

    Mine was about 15 minutes at 4,900 so'm. I charged on purpose instead of giving it away, because I wanted to see whether convenience was something people would actually pay for.

    What Happened

    Doors reached
    250
    QR scan rate
    12%
    Orders completed
    2

    250 flyers → about 30 scans → 2 orders. Two weeks live, another week trying to fix it, shut down at day 30 against the bar I set before launch: fewer than five orders a week meant stop.

    Direct mail usually runs 1 to 5%, so attention was not the problem. Both of the people who messaged went on to order and pay. The whole drop-off sits between scanning and sending a message.

    What I Built

    Flyer with a QR code → a direct Telegram message to the shop. One employee from the partner shop took the order and handled delivery. About five hours of setup and effectively zero cash.

    No bot, no app, no website. The QR code opened a normal chat with the shop, which was the shortest path I could build. Everyone here already has Telegram open several times a day, so there was nothing to install and nothing to learn.

    Bilingual Uzbek and Russian. Full size: front, back, posters.

    What Broke

    1. People scanned, then stopped

    About 30 people scanned. Two messaged. The QR opened an empty chat with no catalog, no prices and nothing to react to, so sending the first message meant already knowing what you wanted and typing it out to a shop you had never written to before.

    Scanning is free and anonymous. Messaging is neither.

    2. What I still do not know

    I cannot say why 30 became 2. It could be that the empty chat gave people nothing to act on. It could be trust: the informal version ran on a known neighbor, mine ran on a QR code. It could be that the shop is a 500-metre walk, so convenience was never worth much here.

    Those are three different problems with three different fixes, and I stopped before finding out which one it was.

    What I Take From It

    Two orders is not a business and it is well short of the five a week I had set as the bar. It is also not nothing: both people who asked went on to pay, so convenience is worth something to someone here. What I could not do was get more than two people out of thirty to send that first message.

    Five hours and effectively no money to rule out a direction I would otherwise have spent a summer on. I set the kill criteria before launching and stopped when the data hit them.

    Attention was cheap and action was expensive. Twelve percent of doors were curious enough to scan. Seven percent of those were willing to actually ask for something. The whole business lived in the gap between those two numbers, and I had no plan for it.

    • Put prices and a short stock list on the flyer itself, so the first message is easy to send.
    • Ask the people who scanned and did not write. Twenty doors, week one, not after the numbers were in.
    • Give people something to reply to instead of an empty chat.
